Farm Fencing around Albuquerque, NM

Farm fence is for animals and long lines: rail, horse fence, and field fence. It is a different product from a privacy panel, and it shows up on acreage in the North Valley, Corrales, Los Lunas, and the East Mountains.

When farm fence is the right job

Choose rail or horse fence when an animal has to see the barrier. Choose field fence or high-tensile wire when the job is cattle, goats, or a long boundary that should not be a solid wall.

Skip privacy panels around a whole pasture. The footage gets expensive, and a solid line catches wind. Use privacy only beside the house or a neighbor, and keep the rest of the line open.

How a farm fence gets planned

The animal comes first. The ditch, if there is one, comes next.

  1. 1

    Name the animals

    Horses, cattle, goats, or a mix. A horse needs a fence it can see. A goat needs a tighter mesh than a horse rail.

  2. 2

    Walk ditches and gates

    An acequia or irrigation ditch on the line has to be respected. A gate has to be wide enough for the truck or trailer that uses it.

  3. 3

    Separate house and field

    Privacy by the house and field fence on the boundary are two lines on the estimate. They do not share a price per foot.

  4. 4

    Set posts and hang the gate

    Corner and gate posts do the work. A light line post will not carry a farm gate.

What changes a farm fence price

Length is large, and the gate and the corners are where the money hides.

Length and terrain

Acreage adds posts. Caliche, slopes, and a line the truck cannot drive change the labor on every one of those posts.

The animal

Horse rail, field fence, and a goat-tight mesh are different materials. The species is the spec.

Gates

A drive gate wide enough for a truck is posts, hardware, and time. It is not thrown in with the wire.

Ditches and corners

Working around an irrigation ditch, or adding corners and braces, takes longer than a straight run in an open field.

Farm fence questions

How much does farm fencing cost?

It is priced by the foot, then by the material the animal needs, the gates, and the ground. A long, straight field fence and a horse fence full of corners are not the same job. We do not publish a pasture rate. The species and a rough length are what make a number possible.

Can you build a horse fence?

Yes. A horse needs a fence it can see, which is why rail or a visible rail-and-mesh mix is the usual request. Barbed wire alone is a poor horse fence. Tell us how the horses are kept and where the gates need to open. We price that layout, not a generic ranch package.

Do you install cattle or goat fence?

Yes, and they are not the horse fence. Cattle are often held with field fence or high-tensile wire. Goats need a tighter mesh and a bottom they cannot slip under. If the property has more than one species, say so. One product rarely does both jobs well.

What if an acequia or ditch crosses the fence line?

The fence has to respect the ditch and its easement. We do not block a ditch to make a straight line, and we do not decide the easement. If water runs through the property, show us before we price the posts. Corrales, Los Ranchos, and other valley lots run into this more than a mesa subdivision does.

Can you put a truck gate in a farm fence?

Yes. Tell us the width before we price the hinge posts. A light line post will not carry that leaf, and the gate is a large part of a pasture job. A walk gate and a drive gate are separate lines. Measure the equipment, not just the people.

Should a large lot be privacy fence all the way around?

Usually no. Privacy fence by the foot gets expensive on a long boundary, and a solid line catches wind. Most acreage needs privacy only beside the house or a neighbor. The rest of the line can be rail or field fence. We would rather price the screen where you want a screen.
